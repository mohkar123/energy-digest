# UK market context

## Regulators and operators
- **Ofgem** is the regulator: the quarterly price cap, consultations, network price controls (RIIO, including the next transmission and distribution periods), and settlement/market-reform decisions.
- **NESO** (National Energy System Operator) runs the system and publishes Future Energy Scenarios and demand forecasts.
- **Elexon** administers the Balancing and Settlement Code (BSC) and manages Market-wide Half-Hourly Settlement (MHHS) delivery.
- **DESNZ** sets energy policy: clean power targets, heat-pump and EV policy, the Capacity Market framework.
- Distribution network operators (DNOs) and suppliers are the main buyers.

## Key mechanisms
- **MHHS:** moves all electricity meters from estimated or profiled settlement to half-hourly settlement. It increases the need for accurate half-hourly demand forecasting and settlement estimation at suppliers. Migration is phased, and milestones have been re-baselined before; check the latest Elexon programme plan (verify).
- **Price cap:** set quarterly by Ofgem; useful for demand-response and tariff behaviour context, but rarely a direct Forecast driver.
- **RIIO network price controls:** DNO load-related expenditure depends on demand forecasts; planning-profile and LTF relevance is high.
- **Capacity Market auctions:** T-1 and T-4 auctions procure capacity; parameters depend on NESO demand forecasts.
- **Time-of-use tariffs, smart-meter rollout, and flexibility markets** change load shape.

## Recurring calendar anchors
- Ofgem price cap announcements (quarterly; effective dates start of Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct).
- NESO Future Energy Scenarios (typically mid-year; verify) and winter/summer outlooks.
- Capacity Market auctions (early in the year for T-4 and T-1; verify the schedule).
- Elexon MHHS migration milestone reviews and BSC change panels.
- Ofgem RIIO consultation stages.

## What a signal looks like for a load-forecasting product
- An MHHS milestone shift, a BSC change, or a supplier readiness or performance report that affects suppliers.
- Ofgem or NESO decisions on demand forecasting methodology, flexibility markets, or large-load connections reform (data centres, connection queue).
- DNO plans showing changes to demand forecasts, EV or heat-pump uptake assumptions.
- Government heat-pump, EV mandate, or smart-meter policy changes.
- Competitor activity among UK forecasting and settlement vendors.
