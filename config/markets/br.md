# Brazil market context

## Regulators and operators
- **ANEEL** is the regulator: annual tariff adjustments (reajuste tarifario) per distributor, periodic tariff reviews, and the monthly tariff flags (bandeiras tarifarias: green, yellow, red levels 1 and 2).
- **ONS** is the national system operator; it schedules and dispatches the grid and produces load forecasts used in dispatch.
- **CCEE** operates the wholesale market and calculates the PLD (settlement price for differences), published at least weekly and by sub-market.
- **MME** (ministry) and **EPE** (energy research company) set policy and planning, including the ten-year expansion plan (PDE) and demand studies.
- Distributors respond to ANEEL tariff processes.

## Key mechanisms
- **Market opening:** Law 15,269/2025 reforms the sector and opens the free market progressively to more consumers (verify details and phasing).
- **PLD exposure:** distributors and retailers carry price risk on forecast errors; better STF reduces exposure.
- **LRCAP battery auctions:** capacity-reserve auctions that include BESS (verify current schedule and status).
- **Curtailment:** wind and solar curtailment (constrained-off) has become a major issue and affects net load and DER visibility.
- **Distributed generation:** very high rooftop solar uptake makes behind-the-meter detection and net-load forecasting valuable.
- **Tariff structure:** time-of-use white tariff and changes to DG compensation affect load shape.

## Recurring calendar anchors
- Monthly bandeira announcement (usually late each month).
- Weekly PLD publication by CCEE.
- Per-distributor annual tariff adjustment dates, spread through the year; each distributor has its own dates (verify).
- Dry season (roughly May to November) and reservoir levels; summer peak (Jan to Mar) driven by cooling.
- Capacity auction and PDE publication windows (verify).

## What a signal looks like for a load-forecasting product
- A rule or schedule change for BESS auctions, ONS scheduling, or CCEE settlement that affects forecast accuracy requirements.
- ANEEL decisions on distributed generation, tariffs, or market opening affecting distributors.
- Curtailment orders or compensation decisions.
- Heat waves or demand peaks setting records; ONS load forecast revisions.
- Competitor or partner moves in Brazil load-forecasting.
