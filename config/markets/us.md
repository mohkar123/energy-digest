# US market context

## Regulators and operators
- **FERC** regulates wholesale markets and interstate transmission; large-load interconnection and co-location rulemakings matter for LTF step-change ledgers.
- **State PUCs** set retail rates and approve IRPs and rate cases. Most US investor-owned utilities answer to these.
- **RTOs/ISOs:** PJM, ERCOT (state-regulated, outside FERC jurisdiction for most purposes), CAISO, MISO, NYISO, ISO-NE. Some federal and public-power utilities sit outside RTO membership.

## Key mechanisms
- **PJM:** capacity auctions (Reliability Pricing Model), 5CP coincident-peak transmission and capacity tag allocation, large-load forecast revisions in the annual load forecast report.
- **ERCOT:** 4CP transmission cost allocation (four summer coincident peaks, June to September), RTC+B go-live (real-time co-optimisation plus batteries; confirm the current status, verify), large-load interconnection rules (SB 6 implementation, verify), Long-Term Load Forecast.
- **CAISO / MISO / NYISO / ISO-NE:** day-ahead market timelines, resource adequacy or capacity constructs, and settlement rule changes affecting load scheduling and wholesale settlements.
- **Rate cases:** test-year sales and peak forecasts, weather normalisation, decoupling and large-load tariffs (minimum take, collateral, contract term).
- **IRPs:** load-growth assumptions, data-centre pipelines and resource plans; filed on multi-year cycles by state.

## Recurring calendar anchors
- PJM base residual auction results and annual load forecast (typically winter; verify dates each year).
- ERCOT 4CP window: summer, June to September; alerts matter most late June to August.
- Summer peak and winter storm seasons drive STF and CP-alert relevance.
- Utility rate-case filings and IRP updates are staggered by state; check docket calendars.
- Quarterly earnings calls are where utilities revise data-centre load pipelines.

## What a signal looks like for a load-forecasting product
- A watchlist account, or a utility with a similar profile, files a rate case or IRP with a changed load forecast, or announces a data-centre or large-load tariff.
- A rule change to PJM 5CP, ERCOT 4CP, capacity pricing, or ERCOT RTC+B settlement.
- Interconnection queue numbers for data centres, or a utility raising its peak forecast materially.
- EV or heat-pump policy changes (incentives, mandates) shifting adoption trajectories.
- Competitor wins in load forecasting or load disaggregation at US utilities.
- Extreme-weather events setting new peaks or producing forecast misses.
