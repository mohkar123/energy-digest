# New Zealand market context

## Regulators and operators
- **Electricity Authority (EA):** wholesale market and Code rules; Code amendments, market reviews, and consultations (for example, on distributor pricing, flexibility, and retail).
- **Commerce Commission:** economic regulation of lines companies (distributors) and Transpower. Sets default price-quality paths (DPP) and bespoke customised price-quality paths (CPP); reviews asset management plan (AMP) disclosures.
- **Transpower:** grid owner and system operator; publishes demand forecasts, security of supply assessments and transmission planning (for example, Whakamana i te Mauri Hiko electrification scenarios).
- **MBIE** and the Minister of Energy: policy, gas and LNG, and fuel-security decisions.
- Lines companies (distributors) and retailers.

## Key mechanisms
- **DPP resets:** five-year price-quality paths; the current period started in April 2025 (verify). Forecast relevance: capex and demand assumptions in AMPs.
- **AMPs:** published annually by distributors, using demand forecasts and electrification scenarios; ties directly to LTF and planning profiles.
- **Dry-year and hydro risk:** hydro storage and gas supply shortfalls drive winter prices and security-of-supply concerns; affect demand response and electrification pace.
- **Electrification:** EV uptake, process heat decarbonisation (including coal and gas conversion), and heat pumps are the key load growth drivers, together with data-centre builds.
- **Smart meter data and ICP-level settlement:** half-hourly consumption data supports STF and disaggregation.

## Recurring calendar anchors
- AMPs are published annually (typically around March to April; verify).
- Transpower security of supply assessments and updates (periodic; verify).
- Winter hydro and gas storage updates (May to September).
- Commerce Commission DPP reset consultations and determinations (five-year cycle).
- EA Code consultation rounds through the year.

## What a signal looks like for a load-forecasting product
- Commerce Commission or EA decisions on distributor planning, pricing, flexibility, or demand forecast disclosure that touch lines companies.
- Transpower scenario or demand forecast updates, big-load connection announcements (data centres, process-heat electrification, EV charging hubs).
- Dry-year, outage, or peak-demand events affecting the network.
- Government EV or electrification policy moves.
- Peer lines companies awarding forecasting work, or competitor activity.
